Free Alkali (FA)

Principle: The soap is dissolved in hot neutralized 96% ethanol, and any free caustic alkali is titrated with standardized HCl in alcohol until the pink color disappears. (IBM 1136)
Preparation steps:
  1. Weigh 2 g of the sample into a 250 mL conical flask fitted with a reflux condenser.
  2. Add a few anti-bumping granules and ~100 mL of neutralized 96% ethanol.
  3. Heat in a boiling water bath, shaking frequently, until the soap fully dissolves.
  4. Titrate while still hot with standardized HCl until the pink color just disappears.
FA% = (N × V × 4) / WT

Free Fatty Acid (FFA)

Principle: Same dissolution as the FA test, but here the free fatty acids are titrated with standardized NaOH using phenolphthalein — the opposite endpoint direction (titrate until pink appears, not disappears). (IBM 1137)
Preparation steps:
  1. Weigh 2 g of the sample into a 250 mL conical flask fitted with a reflux condenser.
  2. Add a few anti-bumping granules and ~100 mL of neutralized 96% ethanol.
  3. Heat in a boiling water bath, shaking frequently, until the soap fully dissolves.
  4. Titrate while still hot with standardized NaOH + phenolphthalein until pink just appears.
FFA% = (N × V × 25.6) / WT

Brine Sample

Principle: Chloride in the brine/lye sample is titrated directly with standardized AgNO₃ using potassium chromate as indicator (Mohr method). Silver ions first precipitate the chloride as white silver chloride; once all chloride is consumed, the excess Ag⁺ reacts with the chromate to form a reddish-brown silver chromate, marking the endpoint.
Preparation steps:
  1. Weigh 0.1 g of the sample into a 250 mL beaker.
  2. Add 25 mL of distilled water.
  3. Add 2–3 mL of potassium chromate indicator.
  4. Titrate with standardized AgNO₃ until the color changes from yellow to a permanent brick-red/orange tint.
Cl⁻ (%) = (N × V × 5.85) / WT

Chloride in Soap

Principle: Fatty acids are precipitated as calcium salts, filtered off, and the chloride remaining in solution is titrated with standardized AgNO₃ using potassium chromate indicator (Mohr method). (IBM 1135, AOCS Da 9-48)
Preparation steps:
  1. Weigh 5 g of the sample and dissolve in hot water in a 250 mL beaker.
  2. Add 20 mL of 20% Calcium Nitrate solution and mix thoroughly.
  3. Cool, filter into a 250 mL volumetric flask, washing the filter free of chlorides, and make up to the mark.
  4. Take a 100 mL aliquot, neutralize to methyl orange with dilute nitric acid, then titrate with standardized AgNO₃ using potassium chromate indicator. Run a blank the same way.
Cl⁻ = (N × V × 14.6) / WT

Glycerine (Gly)

Principle: Sodium metaperiodate oxidizes glycerine's vicinal OH groups to formic acid. The sample and a blank are neutralized to pH 7, treated with periodate, then ethylene glycol destroys the periodate excess. Both are titrated to a pH endpoint with standardized NaOH — the difference in volume (sample − blank) is proportional to the glycerine present. (IBM 1147, AOCS Da 23-56)
Preparation steps:
  1. Weigh 5 g of the sample into 100 mL distilled water (Iodine flask); prepare a blank with 100 mL distilled water only.
  2. Add 25 mL of 20% Magnesium Nitrate to both, then filter.
  3. Add Bromothymol Blue indicator; neutralize both to pH 7.0 with 0.1N H₂SO₄/NaOH.
  4. Add 50 mL Sodium Periodate solution to both; keep in the dark for 30 minutes.
  5. Add 10 mL of 50% Ethylene Glycol; keep in the dark for 20 minutes more.
  6. Titrate sample and blank separately with 0.1250N NaOH to the pH endpoint (Blank ≈ pH 7.5, Sample ≈ pH 8.5).
Gly% = (N × V × 9.209) / WT
V = Sample − Blank

EDTA (Metal Content)

Principle: Fat is separated from the soap sample with hot ethanol and HCl, and the aqueous layer (containing EDTA) is pH-adjusted to 4–5. With PAN indicator, it's titrated against standardized Cupric Sulphate until the color turns from yellow to wine red — the copper directly complexes with the EDTA present. (IBM 1145, MPOB Test Method S26:2004)
Preparation steps:
  1. Weigh 5 g of the sample into a 250 mL beaker, add 100 mL of 50% Ethanol solution, and heat on a steam bath.
  2. Add 20 mL of 5M HCl, stirring with a glass rod until the fat separates as a clear layer on top.
  3. Harden the fatty layer in the refrigerator, then separate it and filter the bottom (aqueous) solution through Whatman No.1 paper.
  4. Adjust the pH of the filtrate to 4.0–5.0 using dilute NaOH.
  5. Add 5 mL of pH 4 buffer and a few drops of PAN indicator; titrate with standardized Cupric Sulphate until the color changes from yellow to wine red.
EDTA% = (N × V × 37.2) / WT
